The Documentary Hypothesis 

The Documentary Hypothesis is a scholarly model used to explain the origins of the Torah (Pentateuch) as a compilation of multiple independent sources rather than the work of a single author. Within the framework of The Protocol of the Architect, specifically Chapter 4:8, these sources are revealed not merely as literary documents, but as conflicting religious factions that emerged during the Exodus experiment in the wilderness. These factions were no longer divided by tribal lineage but by their distinct beliefs and interpretations of the Divine Plan.

The Jahwist (J) Source: The Compendium of Caleb 
According to the Protocol, the Jahwist source originated from the group led by Joshua. This faction took the personal name of God (YHWH) and believed the primary purpose of the nation was to establish a Priestly Kingdom. Scholarly analysis of the J source mirrors this, noting its characteristic use of the Tetragrammaton and its anthropomorphic, concrete style, such as God "walking in the garden". God interacting with Moses in physical form modified the traditional understanding of the divine. This group represented an attempt to realize the Four Pillars as a national identity.

The Elohist (E) Source: The Book of Jasher
The Elohist source in this text is identified as the faction led by Korah, who operated under the belief that "holy people make a holy nation". This group was characterized as spiritually attuned and conservative, preferring the traditional understanding of Elohim or El. Scholars identify the E source by its abstract view of God and its focus on prophets and dreams as the primary modes of divine communication. The Protocol notes that this group’s emphasis on inherent holiness ultimately created a theological rift that led to the Korah rebellion.

The Priestly (P) Source: The Book of the Wars of the Lord 
The Priestly source is traditionally viewed as the latest layer, concerned with ritual law, genealogies, and the demarcation between the holy and the profane. However, assuming the P Source is the record of the Baal group, the hypothesis shifts from a study of literary evolution to an analysis of a catastrophic failure in the national experiment of cultural cultivation. In the Protocol, this source is the product of the Baal Priests, the faction that emerged from the apostasy during Moses’ absence on Sinai. While the original intent of the Priestly Pillar was to serve as an institutional anchor for unification and stewardship of the law, this group—led eventually by figures like Phinehas—merged with Baal worshippers to create a parallel system. The Priestly Pillar led by Aaron, which deviated from the intended citizen-temple model to create a complex system of cultic ritual. This shift resulted in social stratification within the community, where certain classes were relegated to positions of servitude. This group co-opted the Pillar of the Priest into a religion worshipping a "war god" defined by violent conquest and social stratification. Consequently, the P Source's characteristic focus on meticulous ritual and sacrificial blood is reinterpreted not as a path to holiness, but as the path dominion. By including the Baal group’s (P) records alongside the more spiritually attuned J and E sources, the final text serves as a "curated mythology" and a witness to the experiment’s failures and successes. P's focus on order and structure served as a "redactional layer" intended to hold the competing narratives of J and E together after the exile.

The Deuteronomist (D) Source:
The Deuteronomist source is associated with the Pillar of the Prince and the administrative necessity for rules to maintain a society. Scholars date the core of this source to the 7th century BCE, emphasizing cultic centralization and strict adherence to the law.

The Role of the Redactor (R):
The final form of the Torah is the result of a Redactor (R), likely a priestly scribe like Ezra, who wove these diverse and often contradictory traditions into a single ideological unity during the Persian period. This redaction preserved the "Words of Tradition" from the disparate factions to ensure historical continuity and to prevent the wisdom gained through the experiment's failures from being lost to cultural erosion.
